# Satellite mapping: lore angles and idea-cluster families

When a runner appears, the crowd trades the surface word. The money for a front-runner is in the cluster around it: the root, the family, the set, the exact quote, the next item in the sequence. This file lists the angles that produced open names in past digs, with the pattern and a sanitized example for each. Numbers in examples were snapshots at the time; never reuse them as live data.

## 1. How to run a satellite map

1. Take the runner. Write one line: what is the story, who is in it, where did it happen, what is the exact phrase people repeat, what object or image carries it.
2. Read the primary source, not the screenshot: the blog post, the filing (and its page number), the store page, the billboard photo, the site's how-page, the product docs, the video's last frame.
3. Enumerate candidates from every family in section 3 and the non-English angle in section 4. Aim for 15 to 30.
4. Run `scripts/check_ticker.py` on all of them. Keep EMPTY and DUST.
5. Run `scripts/presence_check.py` on the top 5 and search X for "$TICKER" in the last hour. If someone is prepping it, say "launch now or drop".
6. Rank with the forge score. Say which to mint if one, which two if two.
7. Put every open name into the ledger with the runner as its trigger.

Time budget: a satellite map should be done within 10 minutes of spotting the runner. After an hour the cluster is usually farmed.

## 2. Lore angles (what CT is missing)

These are the questions LORE asks about every runner. Each one has produced a clean open name before.

- Who is the actual person? CT traded a politician's bunny edit; the real story had a named activist, a specific date and a specific caption. The caption and the real timeline were open.
- Who made the image? CT traded two animal coins off the same art; the artist's handle and site were untouched. The creator or root name is often the strongest satellite.
- What is the real meaning of the name? "草" means grass and is Japanese for "lol"; "NMS" in a filing meant National Market System while CT read "Not My Stock"; a dog named "Cát" meant luck, not cat. The real meaning is a name.
- When did it really happen? An arrest happened in August; the story went global in October. Timeline corrections keep the user from launching into a story that already peaked.
- What is the exact quote? The paraphrase printed; the exact words from the document or post were open ("behavior resembling blackmail", "really really low odds", "intimate and embarrassing").
- What page, what section? Complaint page numbers, FAQ PDFs behind a QR code, the last paragraph of a filing where the company opts out of its own stock.
- What did the account do, not say? A deleted repost, a two-minute RT and un-RT, a display-name change on a founder account, a bio changed to "unemployed".
- What is on the physical object? Building wraps, kiosks, blimps, plane banners, museum displays, newspaper ads, stage banners. Slogans on physical ads are open more often than the brand.
- What is the official page nobody read? A pad's landing page characters, a lab's model-naming table, a company's STARMIND-style product page behind a banner line, the docs page that names the next game.
- What is the next date? Contest sign-ups closing at a specific hour, a hearing date, a court-ordered deadline, an embargo lift, a preload date, a delivery date, a birthday event, a festival the owner said they would still attend.
- Who should get the fees? CT asked for fees to go to the owner; nobody minted the owner. (Only when the person has opted in; otherwise this is a story, not a fee route. See guardrails.)
- What does the clip actually show? A four-second video of a dog at a table was not named by the poster; the name CT used was unconfirmed. Report unconfirmed names as unconfirmed.

## 3. Idea-cluster families with examples

Each family: pattern, then an example of how it produced an open name.

- Root or creator: the artist behind the knight-cat art; the dev who reverse-engineered a platform; the account that found the leak.
- Family or parents: three baby capybaras ran; the parents' names formed a blessing phrase and were open.
- The set or location: the interview was on a plane; the plane was open while the person was taken.
- The fan army: the singer was taken; the fan-army word was open.
- The object: a possessed teddy bear in the clip; the lipstick brand on a building wrap; the saury the owner bought for the cat.
- The verb: "opt out", "reset", "preload", "repost deleted", "unretweet".
- The punchline unit: "five days" (a tenure), "one week" (a boycott), "block zero", "two neurons".
- The quote: "inexcusable, vile", "still no response", "family quarterly meeting", "shitposting multiplanetary".
- The catalyst unit: "objection" (each new corporate filing), "adopting" (each new listing), "boo bucket" (seasonal).
- The venue or villain: the exchange JV everyone objected to; the stock-token platform; the pad being boycotted.
- The person's title or role: "hell maiden", "press secretary", "judge model", "head of product".
- The number or date: nine-one-nine banners that also read as a launch date; a $1 coin; a 250th anniversary.
- The next in sequence: cat then dog then owl or fox; first game then second game named in the docs; this model codename then the next codename on the list; HomePod then HomeView.
- The product string itself: the leaked model ID, the satellite product name in the banner, the orbit name, the factory name.
- The backwards or inverted form: a word spelled backwards launched as a satellite by the platform team itself; "no cut", "no vamps", "no bundle" as inversions of pad complaints.
- The promise on the how-page: each bullet on a pad's "why us" page is a coin name waiting (no block-0, no dev bag, fee burn, "better launch").
- The mascot and the characters: the pad's own clay traders and jetpack hero on its landing page; the game's in-world gator; the founder's drawn pfp.
- The hangover play: the free block party three days after launch; the "bayo" after the "vice night". Only with a fresh word.

## 4. Non-English angle

Every English laugh word was minted within an hour of a paired-launch meta; no other language was. Check the same word in Spanish (jajaja), Portuguese (huehue, kkkk), Japanese (草, 笑), Korean (ㅋㅋㅋ, kekeke), Thai (555), French (mdr), German (nutzlos for useless), Chinese (无用, and nicknames like the one Chinese fans gave a US streamer), Vietnamese (anh yêu em), Italian, Arabic, Hausa, Yoruba, Igbo, Swahili where the story touches those audiences. A Chinese-nickname coin ran to eight figures on BSC while English CT had nothing. Launch non-English angles on the pad where that audience trades (four.meme for the BSC Chinese crowd, bonk.fun for a bonk-adjacent meme, pump.fun otherwise).

Rules: the display name carries the original script; the ticker is the romanization or the script if the platform allows it; the bio translates the joke in one line for English buyers.

## 5. Platform-native angles (paired launches, mascots, how-pages)

- Paired launches: when coins launch paired with a creator coin or a pad token instead of SOL, every buy also buys the parent. Satellites in that meta must use the same pairing (and the real parent CA, not a dust copy) or they miss the flow. Say which parent CA is the real one.
- Pad mascots and agents: when a pad introduces a mascot or an AI agent as its "official" satellite, the next satellites are the things that agent does (fills the form, finds the coin, adopts the coin).
- How-page bullets and site copy: the pad's own words are the cluster. Quote them exactly.
- Creator-fee routing: a dev who accepts fees only to a GitHub or only to one handle defines the only endorsed route. Everything else is unendorsed.
- Trading contests and sign-up deadlines: the deadline hour is the window close for the opposing pad's meta. Put it in WAT in every satellite map of that meta.

## 6. Sequencing a cluster

- Mint the root or the exact promise first (the strongest, least obvious name), then the catalyst unit, then the set or object. Animals and reaction words go last; they get farmed fastest.
- Never put the parent's word inside the satellite's ticker when the parent is a brand (no "GTA" in the name, no "LAUNCH" in the name once the family is crowded); the clean one-word satellite wins.
- Two per hour is the usual limit for a solo launcher: the first with a real post and site, the second ten minutes later once the first has a chart to link.

## 7. Anti-patterns

- Mashup tickers (brand + animal) read as low effort and lose to the clean word.
- Generic words with baggage (a word that already printed and died, a word that is a famous older coin, a word that is also a slur or an unrelated big project) carry clutter in search and in the feed.
- Names of minors, names tied to someone's death, illness, arrest, or crisis, slurs, and race-bait are not satellites. See `guardrails.md`.
- "Possibly" names (an unconfirmed pet name, an unconfirmed codename mapping) go out labeled unconfirmed, ranked below confirmed names.
