# Timing

The user is in WAT (UTC+1, no daylight saving). Every time in every output is WAT, with the date when it is not today.

## 1. Conversions

- US Pacific: UTC-7 while US daylight time is on; UTC-8 after the first Sunday of November until the second Sunday of March. 10:00 PT = 18:00 WAT during daylight time, 19:00 WAT after. Eastern shifts the same way (13:00 ET = 18:00 WAT during daylight time, 19:00 after).
- London: WAT minus 0h during British Summer Time (late March to late October); WAT minus 1h after the autumn change.
- Central Europe: CET is UTC+1, equal to WAT in winter; CEST is UTC+2, WAT plus 1h in summer.
- Japan: WAT plus 8h. Korea: plus 8h. China: plus 7h. India: plus 4h30. Vietnam and Thailand: plus 6h. Nigeria to Lagos-based events: no change.
- Asian launches, zoo posts, Japanese blog updates and Chinese platform stories land between 23:00 and 07:00 WAT. Prepare the evening before; check at 06:30.

Always state the conversion you used when the source gives a time zone. When the source gives no zone, say so and assume the source's home zone.

## 2. Lead-time buckets

- T-72h to T-24h: prep window. Build the kit, reserve the idea in the ledger, pre-check names.
- T-24h to T-6h: most calendar names get taken here. A name still open at T-24h is minted or dropped, not watched.
- T-6h to T-0: speed window. The kit must already exist. Launch when the first public confirmation appears, not when the mainstream story appears.
- T-0 surprise posts (a founder's one-word reply, a deleted repost, a viral face): minutes matter. Satellite map within 10 minutes, mint within 30.
- T+1h to T+6h: the clone war. Only the OG or a genuinely different satellite gets distribution.
- T+12h to T+48h: clones get nothing. Hangover plays only with a fresh word and a dated follow-up.
- T+3d onward: second waves only on new catalysts (a court date, a store date, a blog update).

## 3. Catalyst units and their clocks

Put the exact clock on every unit:

- Trading contest sign-ups closing (to the hour) end the opposing pad's flow.
- Hearing dates, court-ordered deadlines ("by 10am CT"), appeal dates.
- Embargo lifts, review-code dates, preload dates, delivery dates, "in stores" days.
- Opt-out windows (30-day notice periods) and each company's filing as a unit.
- Open-weights release dates, API shutoff dates, index release days.
- Festival and event dates an owner said they would attend; birthdays of famous pets.
- Giveaway draw times (a coin tied to a giveaway dumped at the draw).
- Keynote start times; the product name is launchable minutes after reveal, not before.

## 4. Habits of recurring sources

Record each source's shipping habit in the ledger notes after two observations: the weekday, the US slot, whether they tease the day before, whether staff leak on the night. Use the habit as LIKELY, never CONFIRMED. A past kit was timed on "this lab ships on Tuesdays" plus a "this week" post; that is a LIKELY tag with two sources, not a date.

## 5. Second waves

- Source updates: a blog post about a sick pet updated; a court filing amended; a billboard replaced; a second trailer.
- Official reactions: the platform replying, the person replying, the company issuing a statement.
- Mainstream coverage waves: US evening coverage (01:00 to 04:00 WAT) and Asian morning coverage (02:00 to 06:00 WAT) can relight a story that peaked in the afternoon.
- Deadlines reached: a sign-up close, an opt-out window end, a delivery day.

For each runner, write "Next catalyst: <unit> at <HH:MM WAT, date>" in the dig.

## 6. The user's day

Default active window 09:00 to 22:00 WAT, weekends included. Full dig at the top of each hour in the window; fast sweep every 30 minutes; morning tech radar before the window opens; evening brief at 18:00 WAT before the US afternoon wave; recap at the end of the window. Overnight: taking-layer stream and alerts only, so the morning starts with what was taken and what is still open.
